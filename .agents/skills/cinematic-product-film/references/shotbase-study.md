# Shotbase launch film study

Researched 2026-10-14. This is a visual reference study, not an asset license or a prescribed Shotbase clone.

## Verified source

The principal reference is [The capture tool your  Mac should have had](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MoWSwxZGk_0), published by Shotbase on 2026-08-25. The [official website's YouTube destination](https://shotbase.com/go/youtube) points to `@shotbaseapp`; the video's player metadata identifies the same channel (`UCpNvHBiUI7427kd4c_P53Vw`).

The inspected video stream is 1920 × 1080, 60 fps, 58.200 seconds; its AAC track is stereo at 44.1 kHz. Research used actual video frames at 0.5-second intervals across the whole film and denser samples at two transitions. The YouTube storyboard was used for discovery and then checked against the encoded film. Reference media remains disposable research material outside source assets.

The homepage also contains short background and feature loops. A discovered 21.48-second silent ocean clip is background material, not this launch film. A third-party AI recreation with a similar duration is also not the source. Use the publisher chain above when revisiting the study.

## What the images support

Most of the middle of the film uses a white field with substantial empty space. Blue and cyan emphasize selection, a word, a canvas, or the brand. Black is concentrated at the close. This evidence does not support describing the whole film as dark neon or continuously rotating 3D windows.

The frame usually has one main object. Scale alternates between control details, a working canvas, and wider context. Unnecessary window edges are cropped. The pointer is enlarged and outlined; activation remains visible. Perspective is used locally when it helps the demonstrated visual effect.

Short phrases sit near the active object, entering or changing a few words at a time. Type is generally a modern sans serif at restrained weight. Exact font identity, spring values, and easing curves cannot be reliably recovered from the film; the website's CSS does not establish the film's font.

**Interpretation:** continuity of action and result explains much of the film's coherence. Many feature beats take roughly 2–6 seconds, with longer multi-step demonstrations. Scale and pace vary, but a meaningful change gets a short settled hold. These are adaptable principles, not a requirement to reproduce its cut order or duration.

## Sound evidence and limits

The original track was acquired, but the research environment did not support model audio perception. Instrumentation, voiceover presence, exact BPM, and audible synchronization were **not** verified. Do not turn them into style requirements. An empty caption response does not establish absence of speech.

Technical measurement of the downloaded encoding returned approximately −11.92 LUFS integrated, 5.3 LU loudness range, and +2.13 dB true peak. These measurements are not listening and are not recommended mastering targets; in particular, do not reproduce the positive true peak.

For an original Atd film, design a new score and picture-driven accents within its silent-product constraint, then measure the master and listen when that capability is available. This is a production recommendation, not an observation about Shotbase's soundtrack.

## Adaptation to Atd

Translate the reference into continuity, readable scale changes, concise supporting words and visible cause/effect. Use Atd's own mark, typography, controls, fictional content and original audio. Reuse no Shotbase footage, music, logo or screenshots in the deliverable.

For Commands, a selected draft can be the continuous object: select → invoke → choose → execute → see text returned in place. A second destination can preserve the original and append action items. The film should make those transformations legible before compressing them into a brand promise.
